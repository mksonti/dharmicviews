# WAQF or WAKF: Deconstructing the Statutory Evolution, Land Anomalies, and the 2024 Reform Bill

## Executive Summary & Core Premise

In a detailed data-driven presentation hosted by Sri Iyer on PGurus, analyst Mohan Krishna Sonti provided an exhaustive historical, legal, and structural breakdown of the Waqf framework in India [1, 2, 3]. Originally conceived under Islamic jurisprudence as a voluntary property endowment (*Wakf*) meant for charitable and religious maintenance, the statutory framework governing Waqf properties in India underwent a radical evolution over the 20th and 21st centuries [1, 8, 9, 18]. 

Through successive legislative enactments--most notably the **Waqf Act of 1995** and the **Waqf (Amendment) Act of 2013**--the Waqf Board was transformed into an unbridled statutory authority with powers exceeding civil courts, state governments, and even the Indian Armed Forces [16, 18, 21, 35]. The Board acquired unilateral authority to claim public and private properties, barred civil court jurisdiction, placed the burden of proof entirely on existing landowners, and compelled state governments to fund and maintain its acquisitions [18, 19, 21, 22].

The **Waqf (Amendment) Bill 2024**--proposed as the *Unified Waqf Management, Empowerment, Efficiency and Development Act, 1995*--represents a necessary constitutional correction [24, 25]. By restoring civil court oversight, mandating neutral surveys by District Collectors, enforcing gender justice, ensuring multi-sectarian and non-Muslim representation, and stripping the Board's arbitrary land-claiming powers, the 2024 Bill seeks to re-align property laws with the Indian Constitution [25, 26, 28, 29, 34].

This document presents a comprehensive 5-page analysis synthesizing the historical timeline (1906-1947), the statutory evolution (1913-2013), the structural comparison of land powers, and a itemized breakdown of the proposed 2024 legislative reforms [4, 8, 16, 24].

### 1. The Ideological Shift of Muhammad Iqbal
In 1904, poet Muhammad Iqbal authored *Tarana-e-Hind* ("Sare Jahan Se Achha Hindustan Hamara"), celebrating a unified, pluralistic Indian nation [4, 5]. However, within six years, Iqbal experienced a radical ideological shift, writing *Tarana-e-Milli* in 1910 [4, 5]:
> *"Chino Arab hamara, Hindustan hamara / Muslim hain hum, watan hai sara jahan hamara."*

By 1930, as President of the Muslim League, Iqbal formally articulated the **Two-Nation Theory**, calling for an independent Islamic state in northwestern India [4, 8]. The legislative expansion of Waqf properties ran parallel to this political movement, serving as an institutional vehicle for establishing exclusive territorial and economic domain [8, 9, 11].

### 2. The Khilafat Movement Miscalculation (1919-1922)
Following World War I, the Khilafat Movement was launched in India to preserve the Ottoman Caliphate in Turkey [5]. Mohandas Gandhi chose to ally the Indian National Congress with the Khilafat leaders, merging it with the Non-Cooperation Movement in an attempt to forge Hindu-Muslim unity [5, 6, 7]. 

This strategy was strongly opposed by major national thinkers, including Rabindranath Tagore, Sri Aurobindo, and even Muhammad Ali Jinnah [5, 6]. Jinnah explicitly warned Gandhi that supporting religious pan-Islamism would embolden radical elements and damage national integration [5, 6]. When the Ottoman Caliphate was abolished by Mustafa Kemal Ataturk in 1924, the Khilafat Movement collapsed in India, leaving behind heightened communal polarization and institutional concessions [5, 6].

## Evolution of Waqf Legislation in India (1913-1995)

The legal concept of *Waqf* (spelled *Wakf* in colonial statutes) refers to an unalienable religious endowment in Islamic law [8, 9, 17]. Historically, a property owner (*Wakif*) voluntarily dedicated land or assets for charitable or religious purposes [9, 18, 26]. However, statutory developments in India progressively expanded this voluntary concept into an aggressive state-backed mechanism [8, 16, 18].

```
| Mussalman Wakf Validating Act     | Recognized rights of Muslims to dedicate properties for       |

+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+

| (August 5, 1923)                  | financial accounts for Waqf assets.                           |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
| Mussalman Wakf Validating Act     | Applied retrospective validation to past claims, allowing    |
| (July 25, 1930)                   | Waqf scope to cover historical/Mughal-era properties.        |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
| Waqf Act of 1954                  | Created statutory Waqf Boards under Jawaharlal Nehru; extended|
| (May 21, 1954)                    | to all states except J&K and Pakistan territory.              |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+

| (1959, 1967, 1969)                | (final cutoff set to December 1970).                          |

+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. The Post-Independence Legislative Surge (1954-1984)
Following Partition, while Pakistan confiscated all properties abandoned by migrating Hindus and Sikhs, the Indian government under Jawaharlal Nehru enacted the **Waqf Act of 1954** [12]. 
* **Exclusion of J&K**: The 1954 Act applied to the whole of India except Jammu & Kashmir [12]. Consequently, while minority rights and Hindu temple lands in Kashmir were progressively eroded, Waqf structures were established and protected across all other Indian states [12, 13].
* **Central Waqf Council (1964)**: Set up as a statutory advisory body under the Ministry of Minority Affairs, creating a centralized administrative apparatus [14].
* **Creation of Tribunals (1984)**: Enacted under Indira Gandhi just weeks before her assassination, the 1984 Amendment stripped standard civil courts of initial arbitration, requiring aggrieved property owners to approach specialized Waqf Tribunals [16].

### 2. The Paradigm Shift: The Waqf Act of 1995
Passed under a coalition government in 1995, this Act repealed the 1954 framework and established unprecedented statutory powers [16, 17]:
1. **All-Muslim Boards**: Mandated that state Waqf Boards consist exclusively of Muslim members [18].
2. **Unilateral Land Acquisition Powers**: Under Section 4 and Section 40, the Board was granted the power to inquire and declare any property as Waqf if it "believed" the land belonged to Waqf [18, 19, 28].
3. **Reverse Burden of Proof**: Unlike standard Indian jurisprudence where the claimant must prove ownership, the 1995 Act placed the burden of proof entirely on the existing landowner to prove that their property was *not* Waqf [19].
4. **Section 85 Civil Court Bar**: Civil courts were strictly barred from entertaining suits regarding Waqf properties [19, 20]. The decision of the Waqf Tribunal was declared final and unchallengeable in standard judicial courts [19, 20].

## The 2013 Expansion: Unchecked Statutory Powers under UPA-2

In 2013, the UPA government led by Manmohan Singh and Sonia Gandhi enacted the **Waqf (Amendment) Act, 2013**, expanding these powers to an extreme level [21].

```

|                                 THE 2013 STATUTORY POWERS OVERVIEW                                |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+

+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
| Unlimited Land Claim Authority    | Board can claim any public or private property without prior |
|                                   | title deed or historical registration.                         |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
| Mandatory State Funding &        | Compels Central and State governments to allocate public funds|
| Maintenance                       | to maintain and develop deteriorating Waqf properties.        |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
| Statutory Directive Powers        | Authorizes Waqf Boards to issue binding directives to state   |
|                                   | administration for audits, surveys, and revenue records.      |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
| Complete Judicial Immunity        | Decisions of Waqf Tribunals could not be appealed in the    |
|                                   | Supreme Court or High Courts under standard statutes.         |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. Institutionalized Land Grabbing Mechanics
Under the 2013 amendments, the Waqf Board acquired powers that Mohan Sonti characterized as behaving like a statutory "land mafia" [18, 20, 24]:
* **Village-Wide Claims**: Entire historical villages--such as Tiruchenthurai in Tamil Nadu (containing a 1,500-year-old Hindu temple) and entire villages in Bihar--were declared Waqf land by board resolutions [20, 21]. Villagers attempting to sell or mortgage their ancestral lands discovered that local sub-registrars were prohibited from registering transactions without Waqf Board NOCs [20, 21].
* **Coerced Conversion Vector**: Because non-Muslim landowners faced losing their ancestral homes with no judicial recourse, the only legal mechanism to remain on the land was surrendering property rights to the Waqf Board under Islamic personal law [20, 21, 25].

### 2. State Financial Subjugation
Section 32 and Section 72 of the amended framework forced state governments to ensure that Waqf assets remained commercially viable [22, 23]. If a Waqf property suffered financial deterioration or structural damage, the Board possessed the statutory authority to demand state revenue audits, municipal development funds, and infrastructure support to restore profitability [22, 23].

## The Waqf (Amendment) Bill 2024: Detailed Deconstruction of Proposed Reforms

Tabled in Parliament in 2024, the proposed amendment bill--renaming the principal statute as the **Unified Waqf Management, Empowerment, Efficiency and Development Act, 1995**--introduces comprehensive structural reforms [24, 25].

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                  THE 2024 PROPOSED REFORM PIPELINE                                |
+-------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+
| Reform Category                     | Specific Legislative Clause & Structural Change             |
+-------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. Mandatory Practicing Period      | Land dedicators must be practicing Muslims for at least 5   |
|                                     | years (prevents coerced conversions or sudden surrenders).  |
+-------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+
| 2. Lawful Ownership Prerequisite    | Property dedicated must have clear, verified legal ownership|
|                                     | titles held by the dedicator.                               |
+-------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+
| 3. Automatic Government Reclamation | Any government property erroneously designated as Waqf      |
|                                     | automatically ceases to be Waqf land.                       |
+-------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+
| 4. Removal of Arbitrary Claim Power | Waqf Board completely loses the power to declare whether a  |
|                                     | property belongs to Waqf.                                   |
+-------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+
| 5. Neutral IAS Surveys              | Land surveys transferred from internal Survey Commissioners |
|                                     | to neutral District Collectors (IAS Officers).              |
+-------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+
| 6. Inclusive & Gender Representation| Mandates inclusion of non-Muslims, women, Shias, Sunnis,     |
|                                     | Pasmanda Muslims, and minority sects (Bohra, Agakhani).     |
+-------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+
| 7. Judicial Appellate Rights        | Tribunal decisions made appealable directly in High Courts; |
|                                     | tribunal judges no longer require Sharia knowledge.         |
+-------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+
| 8. Government Financial Audits      | Central and State governments empowered to conduct full     |
|                                     | financial audits of Waqf records.                           |
+-------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. Ownership & Dedication Standards
* **5-Year Practicing Rule**: Under Section 3, an individual can only declare property as Waqf if they have been an active, practicing Muslim for at least 5 years [25, 26]. This eliminates historical loopholes where properties were abruptly surrendered during land disputes [25, 26].
* **Verified Lawful Title**: The dedicator must possess explicit, verified legal title to the property [26]. Land grabbing via arbitrary oral claims (*Waqf by user*) without documentation is abolished [26, 27].

### 2. Reclamation of Public Assets & Neutral Surveying
* **Government Lands Protection**: Any public property, municipal land, or government asset claimed by Waqf Boards automatically reverts to state ownership [27, 28].
* **District Collectors as Surveying Authorities**: Replaces internal Muslim Survey Commissioners with District Collectors [28, 29]. As senior civil servants, Collectors evaluate boundary claims neutrally based on official state land revenue records [28, 29].

### 3. Inclusive Board Composition & Ending Sectarian Hegemony
Historically, state Waqf Boards were dominated by powerful elite sub-sects, leading to internal marginalization of Shia, Pasmanda (Backward Class), Bohra, and Agakhani Muslims [31, 32, 35]. The 2024 Bill restructures council composition [29, 30, 31, 32]:

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                           PROPOSED COMPOSITION OF REFORMED WAQF BOARDS                             |
+----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| Mandatory Representative Category| Statutory Requirement under 2024 Bill                          |
+----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| Non-Muslim Members               | At least 2 Non-Muslim members on Central & State Councils.     |
+----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| Female Representation            | At least 2 Women members mandatory on all boards.              |
+----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| Sectarian Diversity              | At least 1 Shia, 1 Sunni, and 2 Backward Class (Pasmanda)      |
|                                  | Muslim members required.                                       |
+----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| Regional Minority Sects          | Mandatory representation for Bohra and Agakhani communities    |
|                                  | in states where they reside.                                   |
+----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| Appointing Authority             | State Government appoints MLAs, MPs, MLCs, and Bar Council     |
|                                  | members (removing self-selection by Waqf Board elites).       |
+----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 4. Judicial Oversight & Gender Justice
* **High Court Appellate Rights**: Strips Waqf Tribunals of absolute finality [34]. All tribunal decisions are made appealable in state High Courts, reinstating constitutional judicial review [34].
* **Qualification of Tribunal Judges**: Tribunal chairs (District Judges or Joint Secretary-level officers) are no longer required to possess specialized knowledge of Islamic Sharia law, aligning arbitration with standard statutory law [33, 34].
* **Mandatory Inheritance for Women**: Ensures that property inheritance guidelines under Waqf settlements respect female heirs, curbing gender discrimination [26, 27].

## Conclusion & Strategic Call to Action

The presentation by Mohan Krishna Sonti on PGurus establishes that the proposed Waqf (Amendment) Bill 2024 is not an infringement on religious freedom, but a long-overdue restoration of constitutional governance, civil rights, and public property integrity [1, 2, 24, 36]. 

By dismantling an unbridled statutory framework that operated outside the checks and balances of the Constitution, the 2024 Bill protects public lands, secures non-Muslim property rights, and empowers marginalized sects and women within the Muslim community [25, 31, 32, 34].

Mohan Sonti and Sri Iyer closed the analysis with an explicit call to action for citizens, lawmakers, and coalition partners [36, 37]:

2. **Coalition Resolve**: Key NDA coalition partners (such as TDP and JDU) must recognize that reforming Waqf legislation is essential to prevent indiscriminate land grabs that threaten constituents across all states [36, 37].
